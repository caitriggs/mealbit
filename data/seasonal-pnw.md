# What's actually at a Western Washington market, by month

> last_updated: 2026-08-29 · Puget Sound lowlands, Washington growers-only markets
> This file drives meal selection. The planner reads the `season:` key for the current
> month and only offers recipes tagged for that season. **Seasonality picks the meals —
> the meals do not send you hunting for out-of-season produce.**

Two things to keep straight:

- **`season:`** is the bucket a recipe's `seasons:` list is matched against. Six buckets,
  not four — PNW spring and fall are long and split cleanly in half.
- **`peak:`** is what to actually look for and shout about in the newsletter's
  "In season right now" block. **Nothing here is a promise.** A market is not a shop;
  the corn may not be there. Every market line in a recipe carries a fallback.

Year-round at the market regardless of month: storage onions, potatoes, carrots, beets,
cabbage, leeks, shallots, eggs, honey, mushrooms (cultivated), cheese, smoked and frozen
fish, bread.

---

## January
season: deep-winter
peak: kale, collards, chard, leeks, parsnips, celeriac, rutabaga, winter squash (stored),
  Brussels sprouts, cabbage, storage apples, hazelnuts, oyster & shiitake mushrooms,
  overwintered spinach

## February
season: deep-winter
peak: purple sprouting broccoli (the great PNW February vegetable), kale, leeks, celeriac,
  parsnips, rutabaga, storage apples, forced rhubarb late in the month, nettles at the
  very end

## March
season: early-spring
peak: purple sprouting broccoli, nettles, overwintered spinach, leeks, rhubarb,
  first radishes, green garlic, morels (late, expensive)

## April
season: early-spring
peak: asparagus, rhubarb, green garlic, radishes, spinach, salad turnips, arugula,
  pea shoots, morels, first lettuces

## May
season: late-spring
peak: asparagus, strawberries (late May, and they are the best of the year),
  peas & snap peas, spring onions, fava beans, salad turnips, lettuces, radishes,
  garlic scapes at the very end

## June
season: late-spring
peak: strawberries, raspberries, snap & shelling peas, garlic scapes, fennel, zucchini
  begins, new potatoes, cherries (late June), lettuces, chard

## July
season: summer
peak: raspberries, blueberries, marionberries, cherries, apricots, zucchini & summer
  squash, green beans, cucumbers, fennel, new garlic, first tomatoes (late), corn (late),
  basil, chard, beets

## August
season: summer
peak: tomatoes (the real ones, finally), corn, peppers, eggplant, zucchini, green beans,
  cucumbers, blueberries, blackberries, peaches & nectarines, plums, basil, shishitos,
  melons, shell beans

## September
season: early-fall
peak: tomatoes (through mid-month), corn, peppers, eggplant, apples & pears begin,
  Italian prune plums, winter squash begins, shell beans, kale, broccoli, cauliflower,
  chanterelles (the season starts), grapes

## October
season: early-fall
peak: winter squash, apples & pears (peak), chanterelles & other wild mushrooms,
  Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, broccoli, leeks, kale, cabbage, celeriac, last peppers,
  cranberries, hazelnuts, walnuts

## November
season: late-fall
peak: winter squash, Brussels sprouts (on the stalk), leeks, celeriac, parsnips, turnips,
  cabbage, kale, collards, apples & pears, cranberries, hazelnuts, chanterelles early

## December
season: late-fall
peak: Brussels sprouts, leeks, winter squash, parsnips, celeriac, kale, collards,
  cabbage, storage apples & pears, hazelnuts, chestnuts, citrus arriving from California

---

## Season buckets, in order

`deep-winter` → `early-spring` → `late-spring` → `summer` → `early-fall` → `late-fall`

A recipe tagged for the *adjacent* bucket is allowed as a fallback when the current
bucket runs thin (the planner widens by one step at a time rather than serving nothing).
