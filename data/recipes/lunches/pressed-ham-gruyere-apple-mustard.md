**Why it's on the list:** a proper pressed sandwich, the kind a café charges $14 for,
built from four things and a pan. Ham and Gruyère is the classic for a reason; the apple
is what keeps it from being heavy at one in the afternoon.

**The move:** **start in a cold pan and press the whole time.** A hot pan browns the
bread before the Gruyère melts; a cold start pressed under a second pan (or a foil-wrapped
brick) gives the cheese its three minutes while the outside crisps at the same pace.
Second move: **cheese in slices, not grated** — grated Gruyère slides out of a pressed
sandwich; slices stay put and knit the ham to the bread.

1. Mustard butter: 2 tbsp soft butter mashed with 1 tsp Dijon and 1 tsp whole-grain
   mustard. It does the job of both the spread and the mustard, so nothing squirts out.
2. Apple sliced paper-thin on a mandoline or a very sharp knife. Gruyère in thin slices.
3. Mustard butter on the inside of all four slices of bread. Ham folded (not laid flat —
   folds trap heat and melt the cheese from inside), a layer of apple, cheese, black
   pepper, the top slice.
4. Plain butter on the outsides. Both sandwiches into a cold pan, a second heavy pan on
   top, then medium-low heat. Three minutes a side, pressed throughout.
5. Rest one minute, cut on the diagonal.

**Note:** the mustard butter keeps a week in the fridge; make it Sunday and this becomes
a four-minute lunch. Wrapped in parchment it travels well and reheats in a toaster oven.
