**Why this week:** cabbage is the best thing at a February market and this treats it as
the point rather than the filler.

**The move:** **velveting.** Toss the sliced beef with 1 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp Shaoxing, 2 tsp
cornstarch and 1 tsp oil, and let it sit 15 minutes. The cornstarch forms a film that
holds moisture through high heat — it is exactly why restaurant stir-fried beef is
tender and yours isn't. Second move: **cook in batches over the highest heat your stove
has.** A crowded pan steams; steamed beef is grey.

1. Slice the beef **across the grain**, as thin as you can manage — freeze it 15 minutes
   first and it slices like butter. Velvet it.
2. Sauce mixed and standing by: 3 tbsp soy, 2 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp
   sesame oil, 2 tsp cornstarch, ¼ cup water.
3. Noodles cooked, drained, tossed with a little oil so they don't weld together.
4. Wok or heaviest pan, ripping hot, thin film of oil. Beef in **one layer, half at a
   time**, 60–90 seconds a side. Out.
5. Cabbage, cut into 1-inch ribbons, into the same pan on high. Leave it to blacken in
   spots, 4 minutes, then toss. It should still have snap.
6. A big pile of grated ginger and garlic, 45 seconds. Sauce in — it'll thicken almost
   instantly. Beef back in, noodles in, tossed to coat.
7. 3 tbsp chili crisp, scallions, cilantro over the top with a heavy hand.

**Per plate:** the cilantro is scattered over at the end, so the no-cilantro bowl just gets scallions and chili crisp. Nothing in the dish depends on it.

**Leftover plan:** noodles clump cold; toss with a teaspoon of oil before packing and
they'll separate again. Extra chili crisp on the side, added at the desk.
