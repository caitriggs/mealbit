**Why this week:** August corn, potatoes crisped in chorizo fat, and a spoonful of chili
oil with actual crunch in it. It's a skillet dinner that eats like something off a menu.

**The move:** **salsa macha** — a Mexican chili oil with nuts and seeds in it, and it takes
eight minutes. Warm oil, sliced garlic, dried chiles, peanuts and sesame gently until
everything is fragrant and just-golden, then blitz it briefly so it stays crunchy. It
keeps a month in a jar, goes on absolutely everything, and it is what makes this dish
rather than a hash. Second move: **par-boil the potatoes before they hit the pan.** Raw
potato in a skillet takes 25 minutes and browns unevenly; boiled-then-crushed potato
crisps in eight and has ten times the surface area.

1. **Salsa macha:** ¾ cup neutral oil over medium-low with 4 sliced garlic cloves, 3
   stemmed and seeded árbol chiles, ⅓ cup raw peanuts, 2 tbsp sesame seeds. **Eight
   minutes, gently** — you want everything toasted, nothing burnt. Off the heat, cooled
   5 minutes, then a splash of cider vinegar, a pinch of sugar and salt, and a few short pulses
   in a blender. Leave it rubbly.
2. Potatoes halved, boiled in well-salted water 12 minutes until a knife goes in. Drained,
   steam-dried 5 minutes, then **crushed lightly with the bottom of a glass**.
3. Corn cut off the cobs. Dry ripping-hot skillet, corn in a single layer, **left alone
   until it blackens in spots**, 4 minutes. Out.
4. Chorizo into the same pan, pressed flat, 4 minutes untouched, then broken into craggy
   pieces and cooked through. Out, leaving the red fat behind.
5. Potatoes into that fat, cut-side down, **untouched 6 minutes**, then flipped and given
   4 more. Salt, cumin.
6. Everything back in the pan together with the red onion sliced thin, tossed once.
7. Lime juice over the whole skillet. Then crema in stripes, cotija, avocado, cilantro,
   and **a serious spoonful of salsa macha over everything.**

**Per plate:** the cilantro goes on in stripes at the very end with the crema and cotija. Serve the no-cilantro portion out of the skillet first, then scatter.

**Leftover plan:** eats well cold or warm. Pack with the salsa macha in a separate jar —
it's the thing that wakes it up, and it will be on your eggs by Thursday anyway.
