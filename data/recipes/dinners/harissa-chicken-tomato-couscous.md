**Why this week:** cherry tomatoes at the market are absurd right now, and blistering
them under high heat concentrates them into something closer to a sauce than a salad.

**The move:** marinate the chicken in harissa *plus yogurt*. The yogurt's acid and
enzymes tenderize while the harissa's oil carries the spice into the meat. Twenty minutes
does real work; overnight does more. This is also the reason the chicken doesn't dry out
at 425°F.

1. Marinade: 3 tbsp harissa, ½ cup yogurt, 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tbsp honey, 1 tsp each
   cumin and coriander, 4 grated garlic cloves, big pinch salt. Chicken in, tossed, at
   least 20 minutes (up to overnight).
2. Oven 425°F. Chicken on a sheet pan with space between pieces — crowding steams it.
3. Tomatoes tossed with olive oil and salt on a second pan (or crowded down one end).
4. Both in for 25–30 minutes, until the chicken has real dark spots and the tomatoes have
   split and collapsed.
5. Meanwhile: toast 1½ cups pearl couscous dry in a saucepan until it smells nutty, then cook
   in 2 cups salted water. Toasting first is not optional — untoasted pearl couscous is wallpaper paste.
6. Fold the tomatoes and their pan juices through the couscous. Chopped preserved lemon
   rind and cilantro. Chicken on top, ½ cup yogurt alongside.

**Per plate:** the tomatoes are blistered in a 425°F oven for half an hour — cooked, not raw, so they are fine for both plates. The cilantro is a garnish; leave it off the plate that doesn't want it.

**Leftover plan:** chop the chicken into the couscous with an extra squeeze of lemon. It
eats better at room temperature than hot, which makes it the single best desk lunch in
this library.
