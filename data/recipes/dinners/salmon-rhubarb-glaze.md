**Why this week:** rhubarb is the first thing to come back at the market and everyone
defaults to putting it in a pie. Its actual superpower is savory: it's sour like vinegar
but with body, which makes it a natural partner for oily fish.

**The move:** **cook the rhubarb down to a jam, then let it go cold and stiffen, and
glaze at the end.** Rhubarb collapses in about six minutes, and if it goes onto the fish
early it burns before the salmon is done. Broil the fish nearly through *bare*, then
brush and give it 90 seconds. Second move: **roast the radishes.** Cooked radishes lose
all their pepperiness and go sweet and juicy — nobody expects it.

1. Glaze: rhubarb in ½-inch pieces with 3 tbsp honey, 2 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tbsp grated
   ginger, a pinch of salt. Medium heat, 6–8 minutes, until it collapses. Mash. Off the
   heat — it thickens as it cools.
2. 1½ cups farro simmering in salted water, 25 minutes.
3. Oven 450°F. Radishes halved, tossed with olive oil and salt, 15 minutes cut-side down
   until browned. Asparagus added for the last 7.
4. Salmon on a foiled tray, skin down, salted, oiled. **Broil 7 minutes bare.**
5. Brush the glaze on thickly. Broil 90 seconds more — just until it bubbles and catches
   at the edges. Any longer and the honey burns.
6. Rest 5 minutes. Farro tossed with butter, the radish tops (chopped — don't waste them),
   tarragon and a splash of rice vinegar.
7. Flaky salt on the fish at the last second.

**Leftover plan:** cold glazed salmon is excellent. Flake it over the farro with extra
tarragon and a spoon of leftover glaze thinned with a little vinegar as a dressing.
