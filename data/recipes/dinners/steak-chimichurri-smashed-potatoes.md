**Why this week:** new potatoes at the market have skins so thin they don't need peeling,
and chimichurri is the best sauce-to-effort ratio in cooking.

**The move:** **chimichurri is chopped, never blended, and it needs 30 minutes to sit.**
A blender emulsifies it into green mayonnaise; a knife leaves it loose and bright with the
oil and vinegar separate. Make it first, before anything else, so it has time to macerate.
Second move: **skirt steak must be sliced across the grain, and the grain runs the short
way across the strip** — get this wrong and a tender cut eats like a shoe.

1. Chimichurri: 1½ cups finely chopped parsley, 2 tbsp chopped oregano, 4 minced garlic
   cloves, 1 minced shallot, 1 minced chile, ½ cup olive oil, 3 tbsp red wine vinegar,
   1 tsp salt, 1 tsp chili flakes. Stir, set aside. **Don't refrigerate it** — cold oil
   goes cloudy and dull.
2. Potatoes boiled in heavily salted water until a knife goes through easily, 18 minutes.
   Drained and left to steam-dry 5 minutes.
3. Oven 450°F (or a hot pan). Potatoes on an oiled sheet, **smashed flat under the bottom
   of a glass**, doused in olive oil and salt. 25 minutes until the edges shatter.
4. Steak out of the fridge 30 minutes ahead, dried hard, salted heavily.
5. Grill or cast iron, as hot as it goes. Skirt is thin: **2–3 minutes a side**, no more.
   Pull at 125°F for medium-rare.
6. **Rest 10 minutes.** Non-negotiable on skirt — it's a loose-grained cut and it dumps
   its juice if you cut it early.
7. Slice thin across the grain. Chimichurri spooned over, flaky salt, potatoes, salad.

**Leftover plan:** cold sliced steak with chimichurri on bread, or over the smashed
potatoes as a room-temperature salad. The chimichurri keeps a week and improves — make
double and put it on eggs.
