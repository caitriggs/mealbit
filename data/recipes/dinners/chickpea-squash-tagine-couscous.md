**Why this week:** winter squash is the market's whole front table in November, and the
rest of this comes from the cupboard, which makes it the easy night of the week.

**The move:** **bloom the spices in the onion fat before any liquid goes in.** Ras el
hanout and cumin stirred into hot oil for a minute turn from dusty to perfumed; added
to the stock later they just taste raw. Second move: **cut the squash small and don't
stir it much.** One-inch cubes cook through in 25 minutes, and half of them collapse
into the broth and thicken it, which is what makes this a tagine rather than a soup.
The lemon goes in at the very end so it stays bright against the apricots and honey.

1. Dice the onion. In a wide pot, 3 tbsp olive oil over medium; onion with a pinch of
   salt, 8 minutes, until soft and golden at the edges.
2. 4 sliced garlic cloves, 1 tbsp ras el hanout and 1 tsp cumin, stirred 1 minute until
   the kitchen smells of it.
3. Squash peeled, seeded and cut in 1-inch cubes (about 1½ lb of flesh). In with 2 cups
   vegetable stock, 1 tbsp honey and the apricots halved. Bring to a simmer, lid ajar,
   15 minutes.
4. Both cans of chickpeas, drained and rinsed. 10 minutes more, until the squash is
   tender and some of it has broken down into the sauce.
5. Meanwhile, couscous: 1½ cups in a bowl, 1¾ cups boiling salted water poured over,
   plate on top, 5 minutes. Fork it up with a drizzle of olive oil.
6. Toast ½ cup sliced almonds in a dry pan over medium, shaking, 3 minutes, until
   golden. Zest the lemon and juice it.
7. Lemon zest and juice into the tagine; taste and salt it properly. Over the couscous,
   with chopped parsley and the almonds on top. Harissa in a small dish on the side for
   whoever wants heat.

**Leftover plan:** packs as it is; the squash thickens the sauce further overnight,
which is the better version. Couscous in the bottom of the box, tagine on top, almonds
and a spoon of harissa in a corner so they stay crunchy and optional. Good warm or at
room temperature.
