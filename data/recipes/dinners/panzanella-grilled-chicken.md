**Why this week:** for about three weeks in August, Washington tomatoes are worth the
entire year of waiting. This is the dish that respects that instead of cooking them.

**The move:** **the dressing is a sharp Dijon vinaigrette, and the tomatoes are dressed
separately and spooned on at the plate.** That is what makes this dish work for a table
where one person doesn't eat raw tomato: the salad underneath is complete without them —
crisp bread, cucumber, sweet onion, basil, torn mozzarella, charred chicken. Second move:
**the bread is torn, not cubed, and toasted in olive oil until properly crunchy**, so it
can soak without disintegrating.

1. Tomatoes in big rough chunks, tossed with 2 tsp salt and 1 tbsp red wine vinegar, into
   a bowl of their own. Twenty minutes. **They stay in that bowl** — juice and all.
2. Bread torn into craggy pieces, tossed with a lot of olive oil and salt, into a 400°F
   oven or a pan, 12 minutes until golden and crisp through.
3. Onion sliced paper-thin and soaked in cold water 10 minutes to take the aggression
   out. Drained.
4. Chicken thighs flattened, salted, rubbed with oil, oregano and pepper. Grill or hot
   pan, 5–6 minutes a side, until charred at the edges. Rest, then slice thick.
5. Dressing: 3 tbsp red wine vinegar + 2 tsp Dijon + 1 grated garlic clove + ½ cup olive
   oil + 1 tbsp capers, whisked hard. Sharper than it looks like it should be — the bread
   drinks it.
6. Toss the bread with two-thirds of the dressing and let it sit **10 minutes** —
   panzanella needs the soak. Then cucumber, onion, torn basil, torn mozzarella.
7. Chicken over the top, remaining dressing, flaky salt, more basil than seems sensible.
8. **At the table:** the bowl of salted tomatoes, with a spoon. Whoever wants them spoons them
   and their juice over their own plate; the no-tomato plate goes straight from the bowl.

**Per plate:** the tomatoes never enter the salad. They are salted, vinegared and served
in their own bowl with a spoon, so one plate piles them on and the other is a complete
chicken-and-bread salad without them. This is the whole reason the dressing is Dijon-led
rather than built on tomato juice.

**Leftover plan:** **don't pack this one.** By tomorrow the bread has gone from crisp-and-soaked to uniformly soggy, and it is not a lunch you will be glad to open. Grill an extra chicken thigh or two while the pan is hot instead — sliced cold on bread with the leftover Dijon dressing, that is the lunch. The salad is a same-night dish.
