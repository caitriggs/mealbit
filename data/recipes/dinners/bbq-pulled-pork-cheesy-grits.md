**Why this week:** the best kind of shortcut — buy the slow part, make the good parts.
Fully-cooked pulled pork does the eight hours for you; the barbecue sauce, the pickle and
the grits take thirty minutes and are where all the flavor actually lives.

**The move:** two, and neither is the pork. **(1) Stone-ground grits, not instant, and
cook them in milk-and-water with far more liquid than the bag says** — 5:1, forty minutes
if you have it, twenty-five if you don't, whisked often. Instant grits are wallpaper
paste. **(2) Broil the pork until the edges genuinely blacken.** Pulled pork warmed in a
pan is wet and sad; pulled pork spread thin on a sheet pan under a broiler for 8 minutes
gets those crisp burnt ends that make the dish.

1. **Pickle first:** thin-slice the Fresnos into rings, cover with 3 tbsp cider vinegar,
   2 tbsp sugar, 2 tbsp hot water, big pinch salt. Leave them. Twenty minutes is plenty.
2. Grits: 1 cup stone-ground to 3 cups water + 2 cups milk, 1 tsp salt. Boil, then lowest
   heat, whisked every few minutes, 25–40 minutes. Finish with 3 tbsp butter and a big
   handful of grated cheddar off the heat. Season hard — under-salted grits are the
   number one failure here.
3. **Quick BBQ sauce** (better than the jar, five minutes): 3 tbsp tomato paste, 3 tbsp
   brown sugar, 2 tbsp cider vinegar, 1 tbsp Worcestershire, 1 tsp Dijon, 2 tsp smoked
   paprika, 1 grated garlic clove, ½ cup water, a shake of hot sauce. Simmer 5 minutes
   until it coats a spoon.
4. Pork broken up on a foiled sheet pan, tossed with half the sauce, spread thin.
   **Broil 8–10 minutes** until the edges char. Toss with the rest of the sauce.
5. Chard: stems sliced and cooked in butter 3 minutes, then the torn leaves with a splash
   of water, 2 minutes. Sliced garlic at the end. A handful of corn in with the leaves.
6. Bowls: grits, pork, chard alongside, sour cream, pickled Fresnos and a spoon of their
   pink liquid, scallions.

**Leftover plan:** the grits set into a firm slab in the fridge. Loosen them back with a
good splash of milk and a minute in the microwave, stirring once, then pack the pork and
the pickled Fresnos on top — pulled pork is one of the best-keeping things in this
library and it carries the box. The famous move, cutting the slab into squares and
pan-frying them in butter until crisp, is a real one, but it is a stove and eight
minutes: a Saturday breakfast, not a Tuesday lunch.
