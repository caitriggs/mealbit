**Why this week:** November is when parsnips and celeriac are at their sweetest and
cheapest, and a long oven stew is the right answer to a dark 5pm.

**The move:** **brown the beef hard, in batches, then let the oven do the work.** A
crowded pan steams and grey beef makes grey stew; three batches with the pan smoking
gives you the brown crust that becomes the whole flavour of the broth. The flour dusting
browns with it and quietly thickens the stew without a roux. Second move: **the barley
goes in for the last 45 minutes only.** Added at the start it drinks the whole pot and
turns to porridge; added late it stays as separate, chewy grains in a glossy broth.

1. Oven to 325°F. Pat the beef dry, salt it well and toss with 2 tbsp flour. In a Dutch
   oven, 2 tbsp olive oil over medium-high; brown the beef in three batches, 4 minutes a
   side, until deep brown. Out to a plate.
2. Lower to medium. Onion diced, leeks sliced and rinsed, carrots and parsnips in thick
   coins, half the celeriac peeled and in 1-inch dice. All in the fat with a big pinch of
   salt, 10 minutes, scraping the pan, until the edges colour.
3. 4 sliced garlic cloves, 1 minute. Pour in 1 cup red wine and scrape up everything
   stuck to the bottom; let it reduce by half, about 3 minutes.
4. 4 cups beef stock, 2 bay leaves, 4 sprigs of thyme and the beef with its juices. Bring
   to a bare simmer, lid on, into the oven for 1 hour.
5. Stir in 1 cup pearl barley and a splash more stock or water if it looks tight. Lid on,
   back in the oven 45 minutes, until the beef gives to a spoon and the barley is tender.
6. Skim the fat off the top, fish out the bay leaves and thyme stems. Taste; it will want
   more salt than you think, and a lot of black pepper.
7. Ladle into bowls with the crusty bread torn alongside. If it has thickened past stew,
   loosen with a splash of hot water.

**Leftover plan:** this is better on day two. The barley keeps swelling overnight, so
pack it with a splash of water in the box and stir after reheating; a hunk of the bread
wrapped separately. Eats fine at room temperature too, more like a hearty barley salad.
