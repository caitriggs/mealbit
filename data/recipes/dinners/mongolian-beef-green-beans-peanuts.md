**Why this week:** this is the upgrade of a meal-kit favorite — same twenty-five minutes,
but the beef actually gets crisp and the sauce is built from the pantry instead of a
plastic pouch.

**The move:** **dry the shaved beef, dust it with cornstarch, and sear it in a single
layer without touching it.** Shaved steak is thin enough to go crisp at the edges in two
minutes a side if the pan is ripping hot and the beef is dry; pile it in wet and it steams
into grey ribbons. Work in two batches, and **pull it out before the sauce goes in** — it
goes back for thirty seconds at the end, so half the pieces stay crunchy through the sauce.

1. Rice on.
2. Sauce whisked and standing by: 4 tbsp soy, 2 tbsp hoisin, 2 tbsp brown sugar, 1 tbsp
   rice vinegar, 1 tsp sesame oil, 2 tsp cornstarch, ½ cup water, ½ tsp chili flakes.
3. Green beans, trimmed and halved, into a dry ripping-hot skillet with barely any oil.
   **Blistered and blackened in spots**, 6–7 minutes, tossed twice. Salt. Out of the pan.
4. Beef patted dry, tossed with 1 tbsp cornstarch and a pinch of salt. Pan back on high
   with a slick of oil. Half the beef in a single layer, **untouched 2 minutes**, flipped,
   1 more. Out. Repeat. Pour off the fat.
5. Sliced onion into the pan, 3 minutes, until it softens but keeps some bite.
6. A heap of grated garlic and ginger, 45 seconds. Sauce in — it thickens in about 90
   seconds and goes glossy. Beef back in for 30 seconds, no more.
7. Green beans back in, tossed. Over rice, with **½ cup roughly crushed peanuts** and
   sliced scallions. Crush the peanuts under the flat of a knife; pre-chopped ones are
   dust.

**Leftover plan:** the seared beef holds its texture better than you'd expect. Pack over
rice with the peanuts in a separate twist of foil, added at the desk so they stay loud.
