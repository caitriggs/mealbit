**Why this week:** this is the upgrade of a meal-kit favorite — same twenty-five minutes,
but the beef actually gets crisp and the sauce is built from the pantry instead of a
plastic pouch.

**The move:** **smash the ground beef flat and leave it alone.** The meal-kit version says
"cook, stirring, breaking up" and that's why it comes out as grey crumbles in sauce. Press
the beef into a thin, even layer in a ripping-hot pan and **do not touch it for 4 minutes.**
It forms one crisp brown sheet. *Then* break it into craggy pieces. Half the pieces stay
crunchy right through the sauce, and that's the entire difference.

1. Rice on.
2. Sauce whisked and standing by: 4 tbsp soy, 2 tbsp hoisin, 2 tbsp brown sugar, 1 tbsp
   rice vinegar, 1 tsp sesame oil, 2 tsp cornstarch, ½ cup water, ½ tsp chili flakes.
3. Green beans, trimmed and halved, into a dry ripping-hot skillet with barely any oil.
   **Blistered and blackened in spots**, 6–7 minutes, tossed twice. Salt. Out of the pan.
4. Pan back on high with a slick of oil. Beef in, **pressed flat, untouched 4 minutes.**
   Flip in sections, 2 more. Break into big craggy pieces — not crumbs. Pour off the fat.
5. Sliced onion into the pan, 3 minutes, until it softens but keeps some bite.
6. A heap of grated garlic and ginger, 45 seconds. Sauce in — it thickens in about 90
   seconds and goes glossy.
7. Green beans back in, tossed. Over rice, with **a lot of roughly crushed peanuts** and
   sliced scallions. Crush the peanuts under the flat of a knife; pre-chopped ones are
   dust.

**Leftover plan:** the crisp beef holds its texture better than you'd expect. Pack over
rice with the peanuts in a separate twist of foil, added at the desk so they stay loud.
