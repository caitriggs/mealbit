**Why this week:** four pantry ingredients and a head of winter cabbage produce one of
the most flavorful things you can put on a table. It also costs almost nothing.

**The move:** **do not stir the vinegar.** Adobo goes in vinegar-soy and comes to a boil
*uncovered and untouched* for the first 10 minutes. Stirring early keeps the harsh raw
acetic edge in the sauce; leaving it alone lets it cook off and go round. Then, crucially,
**pull the thighs out and crisp the skin under the broiler** at the end — braised chicken
skin is otherwise a sad, pale thing.

1. Marinate if you have time (30 min–overnight): thighs, ½ cup soy, ⅓ cup cider vinegar,
   1 whole head of garlic smashed, 4 bay leaves, 1 tbsp whole peppercorns.
2. Everything into a wide pan, skin-side up, plus ½ cup water and 1 tbsp sugar. Bring to
   a boil uncovered. **Ten minutes, no stirring.**
3. Lower to a simmer, partly covered, 25 minutes.
4. Chicken out onto a foiled tray, skin up, under a hot broiler 4–6 minutes until the
   skin blisters and crackles.
5. Meanwhile reduce the braising liquid hard — 6–8 minutes — until it's glossy and coats
   a spoon. Taste it. It should be sharp, salty and slightly sweet.
6. Cabbage cut into thick wedges, seared cut-side down in a dry hot pan until blackened,
   then a splash of the adobo liquid and a lid for 4 minutes.
7. Garlic rice: cooked rice fried in oil with a lot of thin-sliced garlic until the grains
   separate and some go crisp.

**Leftover plan:** shred the leftover meat into the sauce and pack over rice. Adobo is a
preserved dish by design — it is *supposed* to sit. Day three is fine.
