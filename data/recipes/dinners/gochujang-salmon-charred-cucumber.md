**Why this week:** cucumbers are at their cheapest and most abundant, and almost nobody
cooks them — charring them is the whole trick.

**The move:** cucumbers in a screaming-hot dry pan, cut side down, untouched for 3
minutes. They collapse just enough to go sweet and smoky while staying snappy in the
middle. Salt them *after* the char, never before, or they weep and steam instead.

1. Rice on. Short-grain, rinsed until the water runs clear.
2. Whisk the glaze: 3 tbsp gochujang, 2 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp rice vinegar,
   1 tbsp sesame oil, 2 grated garlic cloves, 1 tbsp grated ginger.
3. Halve cucumbers lengthwise, scrape the seeds out with a spoon, cut into 3-inch
   batons. Dry them properly on a towel — water is the enemy of char.
4. Cast iron, no oil, ripping hot. Cucumbers cut-side down, 3 minutes, don't move them.
   Pull, salt, toss with a splash of rice vinegar and sliced scallions.
5. Salmon skin-side down on a foiled sheet pan. Broil 6 minutes, brush on half the
   glaze, broil 3–4 more until the glaze blisters at the edges.
6. Rest 5 minutes. Remaining glaze at the table.

**Leftover plan:** salmon holds beautifully cold. Flake it over rice with the cucumbers
and a spoon of the reserved glaze thinned with a little water — that's two lunch boxes
built before you've finished the dishes.
