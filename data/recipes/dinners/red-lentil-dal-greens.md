**Why this week:** twelve dollars feeds four people twice, and in March there is genuinely
nothing else at the market except greens.

**The move:** **the tadka goes on at the very end, not the beginning.** Most home dal
blooms the spices first and then boils all that aromatic oil into oblivion for 25 minutes.
Instead: cook a plain, well-salted dal, then bloom whole spices in hot ghee or oil in a
small pan and pour it, still sizzling, over the top at the table. The difference is not
subtle — it's the single loudest thirty seconds in home cooking.

1. Red lentils rinsed until the water runs clear. Into a pot with 4 cups water, 1 tsp
   turmeric, a smashed thumb of ginger, salt. Simmer 20 minutes, skimming the foam,
   until collapsed and creamy. Whisk it hard to smooth it out.
2. Half a can of coconut milk stirred in. Loosen with water to a pourable, soupy
   consistency — dal thickens dramatically as it sits, so make it looser than you want.
3. Greens stirred through the hot dal until just wilted. Lemon juice. Taste for salt,
   then add more salt.
4. **Tadka:** 3 tbsp ghee or oil in a small pan over medium-high. 1 tbsp black mustard
   seeds until they pop, 1 tsp cumin seeds for 20 seconds, then sliced garlic until it
   just turns gold, then chiles and a big pinch of chili flakes. **Ten seconds and off** —
   garlic goes from gold to bitter fast.
5. Pour the whole sizzling lot over the dal. Don't stir it in completely; leave streaks.
6. Cilantro, yogurt, rice or naan.

**Per plate:** the dal is finished with a tempered-spice tarka, which is where its flavour comes from. Cilantro is scattered per bowl.

**Leftover plan:** genuinely better on day two. It sets solid in the fridge — loosen with
hot water and it comes right back. Worth making a fresh mini-tadka for the reheat if
there's a spare four minutes.
