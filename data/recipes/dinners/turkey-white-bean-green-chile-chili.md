**Why this week:** a January pot of this makes dinner twice and freezes for a third time,
and the whole thing costs about fourteen dollars.

**The move:** **char the poblanos over the flame and peel them**, rather than dicing them
raw into the pot. Six minutes over a burner until the skin is black and blistered, five
minutes covered in a bowl, then the skin slips off in sheets. Raw poblano tastes green
and grassy; charred poblano tastes like the whole dish is smokier than it is. Second
move: **purée a cup of the beans with a ladle of stock and stir it back in.** That's the
body — no cream, no roux.

1. Char the poblanos over the flame, turning, until black all over. Into a covered bowl
   5 minutes. Peel, seed, chop.
2. Turkey browned in oil, pressed flat and left 3 minutes before breaking up. Out.
3. Onion in the same pot, 8 minutes. Garlic, 2 tbsp cumin, 1 tbsp coriander, 1 tbsp
   oregano, 1 minute — **let the spices toast in the fat**, don't dump them into liquid.
4. Beans, poblanos, the can of green chiles, 4 cups stock, the turkey back in. Simmer 25
   minutes, lid off.
5. Purée a cup of the beans and broth (blender or immersion) and stir it back. It'll go
   from soup to chili in a few seconds.
6. Lime juice and a lot of salt at the end. Taste twice.
7. **Cornbread** while it simmers: 1 cup cornmeal, 1 cup flour, 1 tbsp baking powder,
   2 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp salt, 1 cup buttermilk or milk, 2 eggs, 6 tbsp melted butter.
   Into a **preheated buttered cast-iron skillet** — the sizzle when the batter hits is
   what gives you the crust. 400°F, 22 minutes.
8. Bowls: chili, sour cream, grated Jack, pickled jalapeños, cilantro, scallions, lime.

**Per plate:** the chili is finished in the pot and the toppings go on per bowl. Cilantro is one of them.

**Leftover plan:** the best-keeping dinner in the library alongside the ragù. Thickens
overnight, loosens with a splash of stock, and freezes in two-portion tubs for the week
everything goes wrong.
