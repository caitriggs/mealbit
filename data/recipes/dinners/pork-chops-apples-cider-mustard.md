**Why this week:** market apples in October are a different fruit from supermarket
apples, and a tart one holds its shape in a pan sauce instead of dissolving.

**The move:** **dry-brine the chops** — salt them and leave them uncovered in the fridge
for as long as you've got (30 minutes minimum, 4 hours is better). The salt draws out
moisture, then that moisture reabsorbs as brine. The surface goes tacky-dry, which is the
only way a pork chop gets a real crust. Then cook to **140°F and rest** — pork does not
need to be grey, and this is the single biggest upgrade available to a home cook.

1. Salt the chops, back in the fridge uncovered.
2. Potatoes halved, boiled in well-salted water until a knife slides in, then drained and
   left to steam-dry in the colander 5 minutes.
3. Chops patted dry, into a hot oiled heavy pan. **Don't move them for 4 minutes.**
   Flip, 3 more. Add 2 tbsp butter, smashed garlic and sage, and baste with the foaming
   butter for 90 seconds. Pull at 140°F internal. Rest on a board.
4. Apples, cored and cut into thick wedges, into the same pan. 4 minutes to browned at
   the edges but still holding.
5. Deglaze with a cup of cider, scraping. Reduce by half — 4 minutes. Then 2 tbsp
   grain mustard, 1 tsp Dijon, ¼ cup cream, and any juice off the resting board.
   Simmer 2 minutes.
6. Potatoes smashed under a glass and crisped in the pork fat, or just tossed in butter
   if you're out of patience.

**Leftover plan:** slice the leftover chop thin off the bone and pack it with the apples
and sauce. Rewarms gently, or eats cold on bread with extra mustard as a sandwich.
