**Why this week:** the highest-leverage cooking in this whole system. One $18 shoulder,
one Sunday afternoon that is mostly waiting, and you have **tonight's tacos, the pulled
pork for the cheesy-grits dinner later in the week, and two portions in the freezer.**
That is the anti-subscription move in its purest form: buy the raw thing, do the slow
part yourself, and stop paying someone else for eight hours of oven time.

**The move:** **make the chile paste from whole dried chiles, not powder.** Toast them
in a dry pan 30 seconds a side until they smell of raisins and tobacco — **do not let them
smoke or they turn permanently bitter** — then soak in hot water 20 minutes and blend.
It is fifteen minutes of work and it is the difference between "pork in red sauce" and
something you'd pay $22 for. Second move: **crisp every portion under the broiler at
serving time, not at braising time.** Braised pork is soft; broiled-after-braising pork
has burnt ends. Reheat the same way all week.

1. Pork cut into 4 large chunks, salted heavily. Brown hard in a Dutch oven, in batches,
   until genuinely dark. Out.
2. Chiles stemmed and seeded, toasted 30 seconds a side, soaked in hot water 20 minutes.
   Blend with 4 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp cumin, 1 tsp coriander, 1 tbsp oregano, a small
   piece of cinnamon stick, 3 tbsp cider vinegar, the juice of an orange, 1 cup of the
   soaking water and 1 tbsp salt.
3. Onions in the pork fat, 10 minutes. Chile paste in, **fried hard for 5 minutes** until
   it darkens and thickens and the oil separates. This step is not optional.
4. Pork back in, 2 cups stock, bay leaves. Lid on, **300°F for 3–3½ hours**, turning once,
   until a fork twists out a shred with no resistance.
5. Meat out, shredded, bone and big fat discarded. Skim the sauce, reduce it 15 minutes
   if loose, then toss about half the meat with enough sauce to coat.
6. **Tonight:** shredded pork spread thin on a foiled sheet, **broiled 6–8 minutes to
   crisp the edges.** Charred tortillas, cabbage shredded with lime and salt, diced white
   onion, cilantro, crema, cotija, lime.
7. **The rest:** two portions of undressed meat + sauce into the fridge (that's the pulled
   pork for the grits dinner), two portions into the freezer, flat in zip bags.

**Per plate:** everything after the braise is a build-your-own bowl, so the cilantro is simply one of the bowls on the table.

**Leftover plan:** it *is* the leftover plan. Broil-to-crisp for tacos, fold into the
cheesy grits later in the week, or pile it on a roll with pickled onion and crema. Keeps
five days refrigerated, three months frozen.
