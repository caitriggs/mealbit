**Why this week:** it takes twelve minutes of actual cooking and tastes like it took an
hour, and it is the best possible use of a large cheap bunch of market mint.

**The move:** **khao khua** — toasted rice powder. Toast 3 tbsp raw jasmine rice in a dry
pan over medium until it's the color of weak tea (8 minutes, keep it moving), then grind
it in a spice grinder or mortar to a coarse sand. It's what gives larb its texture and
its smell. Skipping it makes a fine stir-fry; including it makes larb. It keeps a month
in a jar, so make triple.

1. Toast and grind the rice powder. Set aside.
2. Green beans into a dry ripping-hot pan or under the broiler until blistered and
   blackened in spots, 6–8 minutes. Salt. Set aside.
3. Same pan, a slick of oil, high heat. Pork in, pressed flat, left alone 3 minutes to
   brown, then broken up and cooked through — 6 minutes total. Do not stir it constantly;
   you want browning, not grey crumbles.
4. Off heat, dress while hot: juice of 3 limes, 3 tbsp fish sauce, 1 tsp sugar, sliced
   chiles, thin-sliced shallots, 2 tbsp of the rice powder.
5. Off the heat and just before serving, fold in huge handfuls of torn mint
   — herbs go in last or they blacken.
6. Sticky rice and the green beans alongside. Extra rice powder at the table.

**Per plate:** the mint is folded through and the cilantro is not: larb is a mint-forward dish and holds up completely without it. The plate that wants it gets a handful torn over.

**Leftover plan:** larb is designed to be eaten cold. Pack it with rice and beans; add
fresh lime and a fresh handful of mint at the desk to wake it back up.
