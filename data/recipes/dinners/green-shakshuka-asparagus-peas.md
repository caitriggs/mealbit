**Why this week:** asparagus and peas overlap for about three weeks in April and this is
the dish that puts both of them front and centre while they're still sweet enough to eat
raw.

**The move:** **make a green sauce base, don't just wilt greens into a pan.** Blitz the
spinach and a third of the herbs with olive oil and lemon into a purée first, then build
on it. A wilted-greens shakshuka is grey and watery; a puréed one is emerald and stays
that way. Second move: **crack the eggs into wells, cover, and pull them off the heat when
the whites are *almost* set** — carryover finishes them and keeps the yolks liquid.

1. Blitz 5 oz baby spinach with ½ cup olive oil, a garlic clove, the juice of half a
   lemon and a big pinch of salt until smooth and bright green.
2. Spring onions sliced and softened in olive oil in a wide skillet, 6 minutes. 1 tsp
   cumin, 1 tsp coriander, chili flakes, 2 sliced garlic cloves — 1 minute.
3. Asparagus in 1-inch lengths (snap the woody ends off, don't cut), 3 minutes. Peas,
   2 minutes.
4. The green purée in, plus ¼ cup water. Warm through — **don't boil it**, or the color
   goes. Season hard.
5. Make 6 wells with the back of a spoon. Crack an egg into each. Salt the yolks. Lid on,
   lowest heat, 6–8 minutes, until the whites are just barely set.
6. Off the heat: 4 oz crumbled feta, torn dill and mint everywhere, za'atar, lemon, a lot of
   olive oil. Pita, and ½ cup yogurt on the side.

**Leftover plan:** the yolks will be set solid by tomorrow — that is the trade, and it
is the only one. What is left is closer to a baked green frittata than a shakshuka, and
it eats well at room temperature with extra feta and a squeeze of lemon. Pack the bread
separately or skip it; it goes limp in the sauce. Reheat gently or not at all — hot and
fast makes the whites squeaky.
