**Why this week:** the one dinner here that takes an afternoon rather than an evening.
Slot it on a Sunday. Three hours of oven time is not three hours of work — it's forty
minutes of work and a house that smells extraordinary.

**The move:** **brown the ribs properly and then brown the tomato paste too.** Most home
ragù is under-browned at both stages. The ribs need 4 minutes a side, in batches, until
they're genuinely dark — not tan. Then the tomato paste goes into the empty fat and cooks
until it turns brick-red and sticks to the pan, about 3 minutes. That fond is the sauce.

1. Ribs salted heavily, patted dry, browned in batches in a Dutch oven. Do not crowd.
   Out onto a plate.
2. Onion, carrot, celery into the fat, 12 minutes to soft and browning at the edges.
   Garlic, 1 minute.
3. Push aside; 3 tbsp tomato paste into the bare pan, cooked until brick-red and sticking.
4. Deglaze with a full cup of red wine, scraping everything up. Reduce by half.
5. Tomatoes crushed by hand, 2 cups stock, bay, thyme. Ribs back in, mostly submerged.
6. Lid on, 300°F oven, **2½–3 hours**, until the meat retreats from the bone and shreds
   under a fork.
7. Lift the bones out, shred the meat, skim the fat cap off the sauce (a wide spoon, or
   chill it and lift the disc). Meat back in, reduced 15 minutes uncovered if loose.
8. Pappardelle very al dente, finished *in* the sauce with a splash of pasta water for
   the last minute. Parmigiano, parsley, black pepper.

**Leftover plan:** ragù is the best leftover in this library, full stop — it is
unambiguously better on day two. Pack the sauce separately from the pasta and it reheats
perfectly. Freeze any surplus in two-portion tubs; that's a future week's emergency dinner.
