**Why this week:** purple sprouting broccoli is the great February vegetable of the
Pacific Northwest and it exists for about six weeks. When it's at the market, buy it.

**The move:** **cook the broccoli in the pasta water**, in the same pot, for the last 4
minutes. You get one pot instead of two, and the pasta water picks up the broccoli's
flavor and goes to work in the sauce. Second move: **pangrattato** — toasted garlic
breadcrumbs over the top. It's the cheapest possible upgrade and it turns a soft dish
into one with contrast.

1. Pangrattato: ¾ cup breadcrumbs in olive oil over medium with a sliced garlic clove and
   a pinch of chili, stirred until deep gold, 5 minutes. Salt. Onto a plate — they'll go
   soggy in the pan.
2. Sausage broken into a wide pan with olive oil, pressed flat and **left alone 4 minutes**
   to crust before breaking it up. Toasted crust is the point.
3. 4 sliced garlic cloves, 2 anchovies, 1 tsp fennel seed, 1 tsp chili flakes into the
   sausage fat, 2 minutes until the anchovies vanish.
4. Pasta into boiling salted water. **Four minutes before it's done**, add the broccoli,
   cut into 2-inch lengths.
5. Both drained together, straight into the sausage pan with a mug of pasta water.
   Toss hard over heat for 2 minutes until the water and fat emulsify and cling.
6. Off heat: ¾ cup grated Pecorino, lemon zest, a squeeze of juice, black pepper.
7. Pangrattato over the top at the table, not before.

**Leftover plan:** reheats in a pan with a splash of water. Pack the pangrattato in a
separate twist of foil and scatter it on at the last second — that's the whole trick to
making a pasta lunch not feel like a compromise.
