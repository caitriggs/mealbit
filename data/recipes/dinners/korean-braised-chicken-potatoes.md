**Why this week:** this is the braise for a dark wet week — storage potatoes and carrots
go soft in a red, sweet-hot sauce and the chicken falls off the bone, and it costs very
little.

**The move:** **brown the skin first, then let the potatoes do the thickening.** Thighs
go skin-down in a little neutral oil and are left alone until the skin is deep brown and
has rendered; most of that fat is poured off, so the sauce stays bright rather than
greasy. The sauce is gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, sugar, garlic and ginger whisked with
water. Covered for twenty minutes the potatoes cook through; uncovered for ten the starch
they shed turns the liquid into a glaze without any flour. A spoon of toasted sesame oil
goes in only off the heat, where it still smells of something.

1. Rinse 1½ cups short-grain rice in a sieve until the water runs clear. Cook it in 1¾ cups
   water, covered, 12 minutes, then off the heat with the lid on while everything else
   finishes.
2. Sauce: 3 tbsp gochujang, 1 tbsp gochugaru, ¼ cup soy sauce, 1 tbsp sugar, 4 garlic
   cloves grated, 1 tbsp grated ginger. Whisk to a paste.
3. Pat the thighs dry and salt them. 1 tbsp neutral oil in a wide heavy pot over medium-high;
   thighs skin-down, untouched, 6 minutes to deep brown. Flip for 1 minute, onto a plate,
   and pour off all but a spoon of the fat.
4. Onion in thick wedges, potatoes in 2-inch chunks, carrots in ½-inch coins into the pot.
   Thighs back on top skin-up. Sauce over, then 2 cups water, and bring to a boil.
5. Cover, drop to a simmer, 20 minutes. Uncover and raise the heat so it bubbles hard for
   10 minutes, spooning the sauce over the chicken, until a knife slides through the
   potatoes and the sauce coats the back of a spoon.
6. Off the heat: 1 tsp toasted sesame oil and most of the scallions, sliced on the bias.
   Taste for kosher salt. Serve over the rice with the rest of the scallions on top.

**Leftover plan:** pack the chicken on the bone with the potatoes, carrots and a good
spoon of sauce, rice in the same box. Day two the sauce has gone into the potatoes and it
is better than the night before — a splash of water and a plain microwave, or eat it at
room temperature.
