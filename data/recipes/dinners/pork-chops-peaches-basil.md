**Why this week:** Washington peaches are at their two-week best in late August, and the
thing to do with a peach that is *almost* too firm is put it on a grill. Heat turns the
sugar to caramel and the flesh to something between fruit and vegetable, which is exactly
what stands up next to pork.

**The move:** **halve the peaches and grill them cut-side down, dry, until they are
properly black in stripes** — four minutes, no oil, no moving them. Underdone grilled
fruit is just warm fruit. Second move: **brine the pork for fifteen minutes in salty
water** while the grill heats. Lean loin goes from dry to juicy on that alone, and it is
the entire difference between this and a sad chop.

1. Brine: 4 cups warm water, 3 tbsp salt, stirred to dissolve. Pork in, 15 minutes while
   the grill or a cast-iron pan gets properly hot. Out, dried very hard, oiled, peppered.
2. Relish, made first so it sits: red onion sliced paper-thin, covered in the juice of
   2 limes and 2 tbsp red wine vinegar with a pinch of salt. **Ten minutes and it turns
   pink and loses its bite.** Add the chiles, sliced into thin rings, seeds in or out.
3. Peaches halved and stoned. Cut-side down on the dry, ripping-hot grill. **Four minutes,
   untouched**, until black in stripes. Off. Cut each half into thick wedges.
4. Pork on the same heat, 3–4 minutes a side to 140°F. It is lean — a minute past that is
   a minute too far. **Rest 8 minutes.**
5. Fold the warm peaches into the onion-and-chile relish with a drizzle of honey and a lot
   of olive oil. Taste: it should be sharp first, sweet second.
6. Pork sliced thick, the relish spooned over with all its juice, a huge amount of torn
   basil, flaky salt.

**Leftover plan:** genuinely better cold. The relish keeps macerating overnight and the
pork slices firm up, so pack them together over grains or in a roll with a smear of
mustard. If the peaches have gone soft, that is fine — they are a dressing by then.
