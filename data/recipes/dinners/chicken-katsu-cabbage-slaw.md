**Why this week:** shallow-frying at home is genuinely better than most restaurant fried
chicken, costs a quarter as much, and takes eight minutes of actual frying. This is a
direct restaurant replacement, which is the entire point of the exercise.

**The move:** **pound the thighs to an even ½ inch and use panko, not breadcrumbs.**
Even thickness means the crust and the interior finish at the same moment — the single
reason home cutlets come out either pale or dry. And panko's flakes stay separate and
shatter; regular breadcrumbs pack into a sandy shell. Second move: **oil at 340–350°F,
about ½ inch deep.** Too hot browns the panko before the chicken cooks; too cool and it
drinks oil. No thermometer? A panko crumb should sizzle steadily, not violently.

1. Rice on (2 cups). Cabbage shredded **as thin as you physically can** — this is half the dish —
   then into ice water 10 minutes so it curls and goes crisp. Drained hard.
2. Chicken between two sheets of plastic, pounded to an even ½ inch. Salted on both
   sides, 10 minutes.
3. Three shallow dishes: seasoned flour; 3 beaten eggs; 2 cups panko. Press the panko on
   **firmly** — pat it, don't sprinkle it.
4. **Sauce, no cooking:** 3 tbsp ketchup, 2 tbsp Worcestershire, 1 tbsp soy, 1 tsp Dijon,
   2 tsp honey, stirred. That is tonkatsu sauce and it takes one minute. (The curry-gravy
   version — butter, onion 8 minutes, curry powder, flour, stock, simmered 10 — is
   genuinely better and adds twenty minutes to a night that already involves frying in
   batches. Save it for a weekend.)
5. Fry in ½ inch of oil, **two pieces at a time**, 3–4 minutes a side to deep gold.
   Onto a rack, never paper — paper steams the underside soft. Salt immediately.
6. Slice into thick fingers. Rice, the mountain of cold cabbage dressed with rice vinegar
   and a little salt, the sauce poured alongside, lemon.

**Leftover plan:** **katsu sando.** Two slices of soft white bread, crusts off, butter,
a swipe of tonkatsu sauce and Dijon, a cold katsu cutlet, a layer of the cabbage. Press
it, wrap it tight in parchment, cut it in half. It is the best lunch in this entire
library, and it is the reason the 1.5 lb of thighs is fried all at once on Monday rather
than halved — two cutlets go on the plates, two go cold into the fridge for this.
