**Why this week:** peak corn and peak zucchini land in the same fortnight exactly once a
year, and this is the dish that uses both without turning to mush.

**The move:** the sauce is emulsified off the heat, with pasta water, and the pan must be
*warm, not hot*. Scrambled egg is the only failure mode and it happens in about four
seconds. Pull the pan off the burner, count to twenty, then add the eggs.

1. Grate the zucchini on the big holes of a box grater. Salt it, sit it in a colander
   15 minutes, then squeeze it hard in a towel. You'll pull out a shocking amount of
   water — this is why grated zucchini tastes like something and sliced zucchini doesn't.
2. Cut the corn off the cobs. Then scrape the cobs with the back of the knife into a
   separate bowl — that milky liquid is free sauce.
3. Pancetta into a cold wide pan, medium heat, rendered slowly to crisp. Remove; leave
   the fat.
4. Zucchini into the fat, high heat, 6–8 minutes until it browns in patches. Corn and
   corn milk, 2 minutes more. Two sliced garlic cloves, 30 seconds. Kill the heat.
5. Whisk 3 whole eggs + 2 yolks with 1½ cups grated Pecorino and an aggressive amount of
   black pepper — more than feels right.
6. Pasta very al dente. Reserve 2 cups water. Pasta into the *off-heat* pan, toss, wait
   20 seconds, then the egg mixture and a big splash of pasta water. Toss constantly,
   adding water, until it turns glossy.
7. Pancetta and torn basil on top.

**Leftover plan:** toss a teaspoon of olive oil through it before it goes in the box, or it sets into a brick. Then **eat it cool, not hot** — that is the whole trick. Carbonara splits when you reheat it hard, but at room temperature the egg and Pecorino have simply firmed up into a sauce that clings, and the zucchini and corn are still sweet. If you must warm it, 30 seconds at half power with a splash of water, and stop before it steams.
