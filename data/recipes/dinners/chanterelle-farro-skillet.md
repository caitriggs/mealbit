**Why this week:** chanterelles are at the market and they are the one genuinely
expensive thing worth buying there. A pound is a splurge; it is also dinner for four
plus lunch.

**The move:** **do not wash chanterelles and do not crowd them.** Brush them, tear them
by hand into strips (torn edges catch more butter than cut ones), and cook them in a dry
pan first until they give up their water and it evaporates — *then* add fat. Butter
added to wet mushrooms makes mushroom soup.

1. Farro simmering in salted stock, 25 minutes to pearled-and-chewy. Drain, keep the
   liquid.
2. Chanterelles torn into a wide dry pan over medium-high. They'll release liquid, then
   squeak, then start to catch — about 8 minutes. Only now: 3 tbsp butter. Cook until it
   foams, smells nutty, and browns, 3 more minutes. Thyme leaves, salt. Out of the pan.
3. Leeks (whites and pale greens, sliced, washed properly) into the same pan with olive
   oil, 8 minutes to sweet and soft. Splash of white wine, cooked off.
4. Kale, torn, 4 minutes. Garlic, 1 minute.
5. Farro back in with a ladle of the cooking liquid. Stir hard for 2 minutes — the farro
   starch will loosen and go creamy. A big handful of grated Parmigiano, off the heat.
6. Mushrooms folded back through. Lemon zest and a squeeze of juice. Black pepper.

**Leftover plan:** the brown butter sets hard in the fridge, which is what makes it stodgy cold — a spoon of olive oil and a squeeze of lemon stirred in while it is still warm keeps it loose, and farro is one of the few grains that is genuinely better cold than rice is. Pack the Parmigiano separately and grate it on at the desk. The one honest downside: chanterelles lose some of their perfume overnight. Day two is a good lunch, not the dish you cooked.
