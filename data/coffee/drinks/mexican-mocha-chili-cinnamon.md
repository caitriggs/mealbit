Chocolate and chili is the original combination — pre-Columbian Mesoamerican chocolate was
bitter, spiced and never sweet. The warmth arrives about four seconds after the sip, at
the back of the throat.

**Ganache (makes ~8):** 4 oz Mexican drinking chocolate (already gritty with cinnamon and
sugar — that texture is the point) melted into 4 oz hot cream with **½ tsp ancho chile
powder**, ½ tsp cinnamon, ½ tsp vanilla and a pinch of salt.

**Build:** 2 tbsp ganache → double shot → stir → 6 oz milk at 140°F. Cinnamon and the
barest dust of chile on the foam.

**The move:** **ancho, not cayenne.** Ancho is a dried poblano — fruity, raisiny, mild, and
it tastes of something. Cayenne is pure heat with no flavor, and it makes the drink hurt
rather than warm. If you can find a whole dried ancho, toast it 20 seconds and grind it;
it's a different ingredient from the jar.
